Neural Attention for Cross-Lingual Document Comprehension
Alice Zhang, Bob Martin and Carol Lee
Abstract
We present a neural attention framework for cross-lingual document comprehension. The
model aligns word embeddings across languages with a shared transformer encoder and a
token-level attention scorer. On two multilingual benchmarks, our approach improves
exact-match accuracy by 4.2 points over the previous state of the art, while reducing
inference latency by 18 percent. We further analyze the learned attention weights and
show that cross-lingual alignment emerges in the lower layers of the encoder.
Keywords: attention, transformer, cross-lingual, embedding, benchmark
1. Introduction
Understanding documents across languages is central to multilingual information
retrieval and question answering. Existing systems translate the input text before
comprehension, which introduces propagation errors and extra latency. In this paper we
propose a single model that reads the source language directly and reasons over a
shared embedding space. Our main contribution is a token-level attention mechanism
that reweights source tokens according to their relevance to the query, independent of
the surface language. We evaluate the framework on two public benchmarks and report
both accuracy and latency.

2. Related Work
Cross-lingual embeddings. Multilingual word embeddings such as MUSE and LASER project
several languages into one space. However, they are static and do not adapt to the
document context. Neural machine translation approaches treat the problem as a
translation task and lose token-level interpretability. The transformer architecture
proposed by Vaswani et al. provides the backbone for our encoder, while recent work on
attention visualization motivates the interpretability analysis in Section 4.

2.1 Transformer basics
The transformer processes a sequence of tokens with self-attention layers, producing a
contextual representation for every token. Each head computes queries, keys and values
from the input, and the output is a weighted sum of the values. Multi-head attention
allows the model to attend to different subspaces in parallel.
3. Method
Our framework contains three components: a shared encoder, a token-level attention
scorer and a prediction head. The encoder maps tokens of any supported language into a
common representation space, and the scorer computes a relevance distribution over the
source tokens for a given query. The final prediction is produced by the head from the
attended representation.

3.1 Model architecture
The encoder is a 6-layer transformer with 8 attention heads and hidden size 512. We
share all parameters across languages and train with a masked language modeling
objective before fine-tuning on the downstream task. The attention scorer takes the
query representation and the encoder outputs, and applies a bilinear score before
softmax normalization.

4. Experiments
We evaluate on two multilingual benchmarks: XQuAD-R and MLQA-R. We compare against
three baselines: translate-then-read, static embeddings and a cross-lingual
transformer without the token scorer. All models share the same parameter budget and
training schedule.
4.1 Results
Our framework achieves an exact-match score of 61.3 on XQuAD-R and 58.9 on MLQA-R,
outperforming the strongest baseline by 4.2 and 3.8 points respectively. The attention
visualization shows that lower layers align tokens across languages, while upper
layers focus on query-relevant content. Inference latency is 18 percent lower than the
translate-then-read pipeline.
5. Conclusion
We presented a cross-lingual attention framework that reads the source language
directly and learns token-level alignment without translation. The approach improves
accuracy on two benchmarks and reduces latency. Future work includes extending the
framework to more language families and document-level tasks such as summarization.
